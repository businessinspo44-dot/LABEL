Probability, explained without hype

Odds, RTP and house edge in plain English.

A practical reference for understanding the numbers commonly shown around chance-based games. Learn what the percentages mean, what they leave out, and why long-run averages do not predict your next result.

One useful relationship
96% RTP → about 4% edge

In a simplified model, a 96% theoretical return leaves a 4% mathematical difference. This describes long-run expectation, not what happens to an individual 100-unit session.

A common misunderstanding

96% RTP does not mean “get 96 back from 100.”

RTP is a theoretical long-run average. A short session can return much more, much less, or nothing close to the published percentage. Random variation is part of the model.

That is also why a run of losses does not create a mathematical requirement for the next result to compensate for them.

Why total wagering matters

House edge is easier to understand when you distinguish starting funds from turnover. If the same funds are wagered repeatedly, cumulative wagering can become much larger than the starting amount.

Start with 100 units, wager 10 repeatedly, and reuse returned funds: the total amount wagered can pass 100 even though only 100 was initially available.

Variance changes short-term experience

Two models can have the same theoretical return and still produce different distributions of results. Variance describes how spread out those results are.

Read variance & volatility or use the worked examples for simple calculations.

Use the numbers for understanding, not prediction

Probability describes uncertainty. RTP and house edge summarize mathematical expectation under stated assumptions. None of them identifies what the next random result will be.
