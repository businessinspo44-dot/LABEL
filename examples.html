Worked Examples

These examples use simplified numbers to explain the math. They are teaching examples, not predictions or playing advice.

Example 1: a fair die

There are six equally likely results. The probability of any named face is 1/6 = 16.67%. The probability of rolling an even number is 3/6 = 50%.

Example 2: theoretical return

Suppose a simplified model has a 95% theoretical return. Across 10,000 units of total hypothetical wagering, its long-run expected return would be 9,500 units and the expected difference would be 500 units. Actual results over 10,000 units can still differ.

Example 3: repeated wagering

A starting balance of 100 does not mean total wagering is limited to 100. If 10 is wagered, part is returned, and 10 is wagered again many times, total turnover can become several times the starting balance.

Example 4: streaks

A run of the same result can occur naturally in random sequences. A streak by itself does not prove that the next independent result is more likely to reverse.

Keep the scope in mind: mathematical averages describe repeated trials. They cannot tell you what the next random outcome will be.
