House Edge

House edge is the mathematical advantage built into a game under stated rules and assumptions.

The basic relationship

For a simple model, house edge can be viewed as the complement of theoretical return. A theoretical return of 96% corresponds to a 4% mathematical edge. Real game documentation may use more specific definitions, so the stated rules matter.

Illustration: With a 4% edge, the long-run expected loss is 4 units per 100 units wagered. That is an average over repeated wagering, not a fixed charge applied to every 100 units.

Turnover matters

House edge is generally applied conceptually to total amount wagered, not just the money first brought to a session. Reusing the same balance for repeated wagers increases total turnover.

Why short-term outcomes differ

Random variation can dominate in a short sample. A person can be ahead even in a negative-expectation game, or lose more than the mathematical average. The edge describes expectation, not the path taken to get there.

Keep the scope in mind: mathematical averages describe repeated trials. They cannot tell you what the next random outcome will be.
