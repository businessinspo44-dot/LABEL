Odds & Probability

Odds describe possible outcomes and probability describes how likely those outcomes are. They are related, but they are not the same thing.

Start with probability

Probability is a number between 0 and 1, or 0% and 100%. If a fair coin has two equally likely sides, the probability of heads on one toss is 1/2, or 50%.

Simple example: A fair six-sided die has six equally likely outcomes. The chance of rolling a 4 is 1/6, about 16.67%.

Odds in different formats

Odds may be written as fractions, decimals, or ratios. The format changes how the number looks, but it does not change the underlying probability. In gaming contexts, displayed odds can also include a built-in margin, so converting displayed odds into percentages does not always produce probabilities that add to exactly 100%.

Independent events

Past independent results do not force the next result to change. Five coin tosses landing on heads do not make tails guaranteed on toss six. This is one reason short sequences can look unusual even when the underlying process is fair.

Expected value

Expected value is a long-run average calculated by multiplying each possible outcome by its probability and adding the results. It is a mathematical average across repeated trials, not a promise about a single session.

Keep the scope in mind: mathematical averages describe repeated trials. They cannot tell you what the next random outcome will be.
